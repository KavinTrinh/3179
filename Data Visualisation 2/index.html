Australia's Emission Challenge

A Global Perspective and Local Reality: Looking at emissions, economic growth, and how to get to a sustainable future.

The Global Carbon Landscape

Australia stands out on the world stage with one of the highest per capita CO₂ emissions among developed nations. This interactive map reveals our position among global emitters.

Key Insight: Use the year slider to see how emissions evolved. Per capita figures tell the real story of carbon intensity.

Economic Growth vs. Environmental Cost

For a long time, being rich meant being polluted. But this chart shows that there is a choice: China's path (from the bottom left to the top right) shows that growth is carbon-intensive. On the other hand, European countries show that decoupling works: their bubbles move to the right (richer) and down (cleaner). The uncomfortable truth for Australia is that our emissions are still too high, even though we have the same amount of money as the UK or France. We are stuck with coal and heavy industry while our competitors moved to cleaner models. This visualization explores the relationship between GDP and CO₂ emissions from 1970 to 2020.

Interactive: Use filters to explore by year and population. Notice how bubble size represents population, revealing different development patterns.

Two Decades of Progress

How does Australia's emissions trajectory compare to other top economies around the world? This chart reveals striking differences in regional carbon journeys.

Use the brush: Drag on the bottom chart to zoom into specific time periods and examine trends in detail. Oceania's line is highlighted in bold.

Inside Australia: State by State

Three decades of state-by-state emissions reveal regional patterns and disparities across Australia. Queensland and NSW Drag the year slider and watch the transformation. Queensland and NSW show dramatic lightening from deep red (150+ Mt) to orange-coral tones (under 130 Mt). Other states also have undergone a slight decrement in the amount of emissions through decades

Inside Australia: Sector Breakdown

This line graph, "Sectoral Emissions Trends," shows how the percentage of greenhouse gas emissions in seven important Australian economic sectors has changed from 2006 to 2024. The dashed horizontal line shows that the year 2005 is the starting point for the chart. Each coloured line stands for a different sector, like Electricity, Transport, or Agriculture. It shows whether emissions have gone up (trending above the line) or down (trending below the line) since 2005. The smaller grey chart at the bottom shows an overview and lets you choose a specific time range to zoom in on the main graph.

The Energy Transition

Australia's path to lower emissions runs through its energy system. This chart reveals each state's progress in transitioning from fossil fuels to renewables.

Reading the chart: Left bars show renewables, right bars show fossil fuels. Tasmania (95%) and South Australia (74%) prove clean energy works.
