Australia's Emission Challenge

A Global Perspective and Local Reality: Exploring emissions, economic growth, and the path to a sustainable future

The Global Carbon Landscape

Australia stands out on the world stage with one of the highest per capita CO₂ emissions among developed nations. This interactive map reveals our position among global emitters.

Key Insight: Use the year slider to see how emissions evolved over time. Per capita figures tell the real story of carbon intensity.

Economic Growth vs Environmental Cost

Does economic prosperity require high emissions? This visualization explores the relationship between GDP and CO₂ emissions from 1970 to 2020.

Interactive: Use filters to explore by continent and population. Notice how bubble size represents population, revealing different development patterns.

Two Decades of Progress

How does Australia's emissions trajectory compare to other regions? This chart reveals striking differences in regional carbon journeys.

Use the brush: Drag on the bottom chart to zoom into specific time periods and examine trends in detail. Oceania's line (with Australia) is highlighted in bold.

Inside Australia

State Evolution: Three decades of state-by-state emissions reveal regional patterns and disparities across Australia.

Sectoral Breakdown: Understanding which sectors drive emissions is key to policy responses.

Queensland and NSW dominate totals, but per capita tells a different story.

The Energy Transition

Australia's path to lower emissions runs through its energy system. This chart reveals each state's progress in transitioning from fossil fuels to renewables.

Reading the chart: Left bars show renewable composition (solar, wind, hydro), right bars show fossil fuel reliance (coal, gas). Percentage labels show renewable share.

Tasmania (95%) and South Australia (74%) prove clean energy works. Queensland and NT lag behind.
